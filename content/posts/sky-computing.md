<p>I’ve been wanting to say a few words about sky computing for a while and eventually <a href="http://www.isgtw.org/">iSGTW</a> forestalled me with <a href="http://www.isgtw.org/?pid=1002832">a very nice article on the topic</a>. It describes a cool work by <a href="http://www.nimbusproject.org/">Nimbus</a> committer <a href="http://perso.univ-rennes1.fr/pierre.riteau/">Pierre Riteau</a> who created a virtual cluster of over a thousand cores over resources leased from six Nimbus clouds: three provided by <a href="https://www.grid5000.fr/mediawiki/index.php/Grid5000:Home">Grid’5000</a> and three by <a href="www.futuregrid.org">FutureGrid</a>.</p>
<p>“Sky computing” was a name we coined back in 2008 to describe the idea of operating in a multi-cloud environment. It addresses the issues of provider interoperability and comparison between providers (cloud markets) as well as end-user concerns – the abstractions and tools required to provide an integrated and secure environment over resources provisioned in multiple potentially distributed clouds which <a href="http://www.nimbusproject.org/files/Sky_Computing.pdf">the original paper</a> focused on.</p>
<p>Pierre’s work pushes the limits of this concept in terms of scale: distributing more images to more distributed clouds for longer sustained leases obtained faster. He started out with a <a href="http://www.drdobbs.com/high-performance-computing/224700195">Grid5000 Large Scale Deployment Challenge award</a> earlier this year for managing deployment of hundreds, and by mid-summer was investigating the properties of distributed clusters of thousands of cores. I can’t wait to see what happens next ;-).</p>
<p>More information about Pierre’s sky computing ventures can be found in his <a href="http://www.nimbusproject.org/files/riteau_tg10.ppt">TeraGrid 2010 poster</a> and <a href="http://ercim-news.ercim.eu/en83/special/large-scale-cloud-computing-research-sky-computing-on-futuregrid-and-grid5000">the recent ERCIM article</a>.</p>
</section>
